Philosophy

Three things we build every program around

None of this is exotic. It's just usually skipped in favor of whatever burns the most calories fastest.

Mobility isn't optional past forty

Joint range of motion doesn't hold steady on its own — it's maintained through deliberate work or it narrows, quietly, year over year. We treat hip flexion, thoracic rotation, and ankle dorsiflexion as trainable capacities, not stretches you do if there's time left.

Recovery is trained, not just taken

Sleep, tissue repair, and nervous-system regulation respond to the same kind of deliberate practice as strength does. We program recovery capacity directly — load, then adequate rest, then a measured step forward — rather than treating it as whatever's left over.

Sustainable pace beats intensity that burns out

A program you can hold for three years outperforms one you quit after three weeks. We size the load to what your tissue can adapt to this month, and let the results compound — the way they're supposed to.

How it works

Four stages, in this order

Programming follows an assessment, always — not the other way around.

01

Movement assessment

A 60-minute session covering joint range of motion, load tolerance, movement history, and any injury or pain patterns — the baseline everything else is built from.

~60 min · video or in-person
02

Building your baseline

Two to three weeks establishing what your current capacity actually is — not what it was five years ago, or what you think it should be.

weeks 1–3
03

Progressive programming

Load increases in small, deliberate steps as tissue capacity and movement quality allow — adjusted session to session, not locked to a template.

ongoing
04

Maintenance & adjustment

Quarterly re-assessment keeps the program honest as life, travel, and recovery capacity change — so the plan bends instead of breaking.

every 12 weeks
